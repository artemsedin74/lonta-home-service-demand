# Related work and scope differences

Professional/DIY spending and housing-vintage patterns have been studied before.
Lonta does not claim to have discovered those relationships or collected a new
national household survey. The links below are context, not endorsements of Lonta.
This is a bounded source comparison, not a systematic review or an assertion that
no other publication contains similar calculations.

## Primary context

- [Census: does AHS collect remodeling data?](https://www.census.gov/help/topics/faq.does-the-ahs-collect-data-on-remodeling.html)
  describes the home-improvement module, its two-year recall and existing
  professional/DIY columns in Table Creator. Repackaging those columns alone is
  not a new survey or a new concept.
- [Harvard JCHS: Interactive Tool Highlights Disparities in Remodeling Spending](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/blog/interactive-tool-highlights-disparities-remodeling-spending)
  (April 10, 2025) uses the 2023 AHS to compare professional/DIY improvement
  spending across homeowner and housing-stock characteristics. Its published
  per-owner averages are not interchangeable with our project-level medians or
  a within-service share of projects/spending. The broad demographic and
  housing-stock relationships are prior work, not a Lonta discovery.
- [Census: 2023 Home Improvements infographic](https://www.census.gov/library/visualizations/2024/demo/home-improvements.html)
  provides a national overview, selected project medians and professional/DIY
  project shares. It identifies Table Creator and an Internal Use File footnote
  for an indicated statistic. A similarly named category or different published
  median is not evidence of agreement or error without aligning its universe,
  project definitions, cost filter and source file. This note does not assert
  that every statistic in that infographic uses the IUF.

## What this companion contributes

A reproducible, explicitly scoped view of eight prespecified service categories
from the pinned DataLumos 217661/V2 National PUF: household project incidence,
within-category project and spending shares, reported-outlay quartiles, raw
home-vintage comparisons and pointwise uncertainty. Source controls, code,
aggregate exports and buyer planning aids make these particular outputs easier
to inspect and reuse. Raw survey records are not redistributed.

That contribution does not establish local 2026 demand, paid contractor invoices,
revenue, profit, a causal marketing effect or a new fact simply because a chart
has been redrawn. Use [REUSER-NOTES.md](REUSER-NOTES.md) for the exact denominator
and interpretation limits. The [canonical report](https://lonta.io/research/home-service-demand/)
is company-authored and prepared with OpenAI Codex, with no peer or independent
review claimed. Research output version 1.0 is unchanged by this documentation.

Source pages read October 15, 2026. No original Census-origin binary equality or
full numerical replication of the external JCHS/infographic outputs is asserted.
